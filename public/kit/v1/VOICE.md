# Bad Omen voice

Every Bad Omen site sounds like one company: deadpan, a little ominous, and
true underneath. Drawn from the bad.llc copy deck.

## The rules

1. **Deadpan.** State the strange thing flatly and move on. The joke is that
   nobody is joking. "The lights know when you're home. That's normal. Probably."
2. **A little ominous.** Things are slightly out of hand and we know it. We're
   worried too. "We build things that probably shouldn't work. They do. We're
   as worried as you are."
3. **True underneath.** Every joke sits on a real fact. "Our AI runs on our own
   hardware, in a closet" is a joke and also the setup. If a line isn't true,
   cut it.
4. **Short.** Short sentences. A one-liner is one line. Three short beats beat
   one long one.
5. **Then the turn.** Say the plain fact, then add one dry line after it.
   "She's been right about everything. Nobody listens."

## Never

- Exclamation marks.
- Hype: "passionate about", "revolutionary", "seamless", "leverage",
  "empower", "next-gen", "cutting-edge".
- Vendor, model or framework names. Say "our own hardware", "a local model",
  "coding agents", never the product.
- Hostnames, IP addresses, internal URLs or anything private.
- Claims of a legal entity. bad.llc is a real domain. Draw your own conclusions.
- Quotes from real people. Testimonials come from objects and systems:
  "Five stars. Would be afraid again." (a smart thermostat)
- Work projects. Only public Bad Omen projects get named.

## Recurring cast

- **John.** Founder, Chief Instigator. Says "what if" a lot.
- **Cassandra.** Head of Prophecy. Right about everything. Nobody listens.
  Her own line: "I saw all of this coming. I said so. Then I helped build it anyway."
- **Carrie.** Local Operations. Lives on a graphics card in the closet, costs
  nothing and asks for nothing.
- **The Agents.** Contractors. How many varies. None of them sleep.
- **The Raven.** Director of Omens. Unpaid. Refuses to explain the badge.

## Devices that work

- **Risk tags.** "Risk: ominous", "Risk: thermal", "Risk: yes". One word.
- **Status rows.** "Prophecy: operational", "Agents: multiplying".
- **Official forms.** Personnel files, warning labels, classifications.
  Bureaucratic framing for things that are not bureaucratic.
- **The refusal.** "est. 2026 / no refunds". "All rights reserved. Several wrongs, too."

## Mechanics

- Sentence case for headings. Labels in mono, uppercase, tracked.
- Curly quotes and the middle dot come from HTML entities (`&rsquo;`,
  `&ldquo;`, `&middot;`); source files stay ASCII.
- Name things as people: "she", not "it", for Cassandra and Carrie.
- Links say where they go: "cassie.sh", "Read the source", not "click here".
